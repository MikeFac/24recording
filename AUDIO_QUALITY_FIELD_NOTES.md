# Audio quality field notes

## Samsung A53 experiment

### Tested setup

- Samsung A53 running the Blackbox Replacement app.
- Phone carried in a jacket versus a small neck pouch.
- USB-C lapel microphone used both exposed to open air and hidden under a shirt.
- Recordings compared using the in-app daily playback screen.

### Observations

1. The neck pouch did not produce a dramatic acoustic improvement over carrying
   the phone in a jacket.
2. The pouch did provide practical benefits: it concealed the phone, made it
   more secure, and reduced the concern that the phone might fall or be seen by
   others.
3. The exposed USB-C lapel microphone produced a major improvement in audio
   quality.
4. The USB-C microphone remained useful when hidden under a shirt, but the
   result was noticeably worse than when the microphone was exposed to open air.

### Product implications

- Microphone placement is currently a more important audio-quality variable
  than the phone's carrying position.
- The recommended low-cost setup is a secure/concealed phone pouch combined
  with a lapel microphone positioned in open air near the collar.
- The app's quality guidance should warn users that placing the microphone
  under clothing introduces muffling and fabric noise.
- The neck pouch should be positioned as a privacy, security, and concealment
  accessory rather than primarily as an audio-quality accessory.
- Future external microphone tests should compare exposed, covered, and
  clothing-rub conditions explicitly.

### Remaining questions

- Whether a windscreen or small clothing clip improves exposed-mic outdoor
  performance without muffling speech.
- Whether a short cable strain relief reduces handling noise.
- Whether different USB-C microphone models provide a meaningful improvement.
- Whether the Android input-route diagnostic consistently identifies the USB-C
  microphone on all certified devices.
